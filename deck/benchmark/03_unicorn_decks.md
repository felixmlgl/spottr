# 03 - Early decks from non-YC unicorns and famous companies

Researched 2026-10-13. Method: every deck below was opened slide by slide. Slides were either viewed as images in the browser pane or read from a SlideShare slide transcript (marked per deck). Slide headlines are quoted verbatim from the slides. Anything inferred is marked **[inferred]**. Provenance caveats are stated per deck.

Weighting: seed and first-round decks come first. Series B and later decks are labeled and given less weight.

## 1. Uber (UberCab) - seed concept deck

- **Round / year:** Seed. The deck is dated late 2008 by its own slides: slide 21 is "SmartPhones, Aug2008" and slide 25 says "Iphone dev license applied for Nov28,08" and "Feb1st demo". The Alexander Jarvis page says it raised a $200K seed in August 2009 from Garrett Camp and Travis Kalanick. The two dates conflict, so the round date is uncertain.
- **Source opened:** https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/uber-pitch-deck-raise-seed-capital-investment/ (all 25 slide images viewed). The original is Garrett Camp's 2017 Medium repost, which returned HTTP 403 and was not opened. I viewed Jarvis's copy of the same slides.
- **Length:** 25 slides.

**Slide order (headlines verbatim):**
1. "UberCab" (cover, subtitle "Next-Generation Car Service")
2. "Cabs in 2008"
3. "The Medallion System"
4. "UberCab Concept"
5. "1-Click Car Service"
6. "Key Differentiators"
7. "Operating Principles"
8. "UberCab apps"
9. "UberCab.com"
10. "Use-Cases"
11. "User Benefits"
12. "Environmental Benefits"
13. "UberCab Fleet"
14. "Initial Service Area"
15. "Technology"
16. "Demand Forecasting"
17. "Overall Market"
18. "Composition of Market"
19. "Target Cities"
20. "Potential Outcomes"
21. "SmartPhones, Aug2008"
22. "Future Optimizations"
23. "Marketing Ideas"
24. "Location-Based Service"
25. "Progress to Date"

- **Hook (slides 1-3):** Cover, then "Cabs in 2008" (status-quo critique: radio dispatch, no GPS, "dead-time"), then "The Medallion System" (monopoly, "drivers make 31k"). The slide 3 closer is a takeaway line: "Digital Hail can now make street hail unnecessary". The product concept arrives only on slide 4. It is a problem-first, industry-critique opener.
- **One-liner:** "Next-Generation Car Service" on the cover. Slide 4 adds the analogy "The 'NetJets of car services'".
- **Traction / pre-traction:** There is no usage data. Slide 25 "Progress to Date" is a checklist of proxies: "Ubercab.com reserved + 'ubercab' SMS code", "California LLC + trademark filed", "Iphone dev license applied for Nov28,08", "Bank Account + Paypal account created", "5 advisors & 15 clients now recruited", "Provisional patent filed".
- **Market sizing:** Top-down, third-party. Slide 17 reads "$4.2B annually and growing" and "Top 4 players combined only 22% of revenues". Slide 18 is a pie of market composition ("Focus on Urban service on-demand"). Slide 19 narrows to cities: "Focus on SF/NYC to begin", then LA, Chicago, Houston, PA, Dallas, "This covers 50% of entire US market". Slide 20 gives scenarios: best case "$1B+ in yearly revenue"; realistic "Gets 5% of the top 5 US Cities... Generates 20-30M+ per year profit"; worst case "Remains a 10 car, 100 client service in SF".
- **Ask:** There is no standalone ask slide. The last bullet block of slide 25 states it: "NEXT: buy 3 cars, develop app, Feb1st demo" and "Raise a few million, small-office + GM in SF". That gives a vague amount, a concrete use of funds and a milestone.
- **Headline style:** Topic labels ("Technology", "Use-Cases", "Target Cities"). Takeaways appear only as green bottom lines inside slides (slide 3).
- **Design:** 4:3 white slides, small logo top-left, medium word density (5-8 bullets), stock photos, maps and phone images, and a lot of repeated feature lists (slides 5-7, 11-12). It is long for a seed deck. Slides 13-16 and 22-24 are detail that **[inferred]** an investor could have lived without.

## 2. Intercom - seed deck

- **Round / year:** Seed, late 2011. $600K convertible note sought. Upmetrics says the round closed at $1M in early 2012 (secondary source, not verified).
- **Sources opened:**
  - SlideShare PDF (Chagency repost): https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/intercom-1m-of-vc-investment-turned-into-240m-intercoms-initial-pitch-deck/149297642 (slides 1-8 viewed as images; slide 9 is an agency ad).
  - Jarvis page with 7 slide images: https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/intercom-pitch-deck-pre-seed-convertible-fundraise/ (confirms slides 1-7).
  - Eoghan McCabe's post: https://www.intercom.com/blog/first-pitch-deck/ (hero image of the cover only).
- **Length:** 8 slides.

**Slide order (headlines verbatim):**
1. Cover: Intercom logo and the word "DRAFT" (no tagline)
2. "The team"
3. "The problem"
4. "The solution"
5. "The market"
6. "Landscape / competitors"
7. "Progress"
8. "what we're looking for"

- **Hook (slides 1-3):** Logo, then the team, then the problem. It is team-first on purpose: four founders, "Ran Contrast for four years", "Launched Qwitter and Exceptional (both acquired)". The problem slide is a single dense claim: "Building meaningful relationships with customers is hard / impossible for SaaS providers".
- **One-liner:** None. The solution slide leads with "simple install like Google Analytics".
- **Traction / pre-traction:** The "Progress" slide has no metrics. It states "Product in development and testing since Jan, ready for public beta" and "Private beta testers love the product, many are psyched to try it". It embeds a Jason Fried tweet: "What a fantastic product idea. Wish I'd thought of this". Social proof from a respected peer stands in for numbers.
- **Market sizing:** Top-down with a single citation. "Now: SaaS providers ($21 billion market, $93 billion in 2016 - Forrester)" and "Later: mobile and desktop app providers". Slide 5 has two bullets and no chart.
- **Ask (slide 8):** "$600k convertible note"; "18 months of runway to work on: product-market fit, customer dev, early marketing efforts, profitability"; "Plan to raise further round in 12-18 months to turn up heat on marketing". This is the cleanest ask slide in the set: amount, instrument, runway, four milestones, next round.
- **Headline style:** Pure topic labels in a handwritten display font.
- **Design:** Pale blue gradient, bullets with asterisk markers, almost no visuals (logo plus one tweet), roughly 40-80 words per slide. It relies on the team and a short crisp narrative.

## 3. Buffer - seed deck

- **Round / year:** Seed, 2011. The deck's SlideShare title is "The slide deck we used to raise half a million dollars". Buffer's own retrospective says $450K closed in December 2011; other sources say $500K. No ask amount appears inside the deck.
- **Source opened:** https://www.slideshare.net/Bufferapp/buffer-seedrounddeck (all 13 slides viewed as images, plus the SlideShare transcript).
- **Length:** 13 slides.

**Slide order (headlines verbatim):**
1. Buffer logo only (title slide, no text)
2. "Social, the most important trend"
3. "How do you use social to drive traffic?"
4. "Queue your updates"
5. "Traction"
6. "Milestones"
7. "Business Model"
8. "Social Media Landscape"
9. "The effect of Buffering"
10. "A sharing standard"
11. "Competitive Landscape"
12. "Team" (with "Advisors" and "Previous Investors" headings)
13. Contact: founders@bufferapp.com

- **Hook (slides 1-3):** Logo, then a macro-trend claim backed by two quotes ("Zuckerberg's Law"), then a one-line question slide. It moves from trend to question to product screenshot (slide 4) and then straight to traction (slide 5). Traction sits at slide 5, early.
- **One-liner:** None. Closest is slide 10: "We plan to become the default sharing standard in any app".
- **Traction:** Real numbers, on one slide: "800 Paying Users", "$150,000 annual revenue run rate", "97% margins", "55,000 users, growing 40% per month", "1.5 million updates Buffered", with a growth-curve image. "Milestones" then gives dated checkpoints from Jan 2011 to a target of "1 million users ($3.6M revenue)" for Jan 2013.
- **Market sizing:** No TAM number. It argues from trend statistics: "Of 200M daily Tweets, 55% contain links", "4 billion items shared on Facebook per day". The only market-scale number is the $3.6M revenue target at 1M users. The "Business Model" slide gives unit economics: "consistent 2% conversion", "5% churn equates to a LTV of $240", "pay up to $5 to acquire a free user".
- **Ask:** No ask slide and no use of funds. The "Milestones" slide implies the plan.
- **Headline style:** Mostly topic labels ("Traction", "Business Model", "Team"). Slides 2, 4 and 10 are claim- or action-style ("Queue your updates").
- **Design:** The strongest visual example here. Giant bold black headline, one idea per slide, product screenshot on the product slide, a dark full-bleed question slide, logo bottom-left, very low word count.

## Patterns across decks

Percentages below are out of the decks where each trait was observable. Weights: seed and first-round decks (Uber, Intercom, Buffer, Foursquare, Mint, Tinder, YouTube) first; Facebook, Canva, LinkedIn and Moz are secondary.

**Length.** Early decks cluster short. Slide counts: Intercom 8, YouTube 10, Tinder 10, Buffer 13, Foursquare 15, Mint 16, Uber 25. Median of those seven is 13; including LinkedIn (37) and Moz (36) the median of nine full decks is 15. Intercom, YouTube and Tinder, the shortest, were 8-10 slides and had no ask slide except Intercom's.

**Common slide order.** Across the seed-stage decks the recurring skeleton is: cover; (purpose or team); problem or status-quo; solution or product; market; competition; traction or progress; business model; team; ask. Examples: YouTube (Purpose, Problem, Solution, Market Size, Competition, Product Development, Sales & Distribution, Team, Metrics), Intercom (Team, Problem, Solution, Market, Landscape/competitors, Progress, ask), Mint (Product, Team, Market, Competitors, Value, User Acquisition, Business Model, Financials, Exit). The order of team varies widely. Team is slide 2 in Intercom, slide 3 in Mint, near the end in Buffer (12 of 13), YouTube (9 of 10), LinkedIn (35 of 37), and almost absent in Foursquare (a trivia line on slide 15), Uber and Tinder.

**Problem-first vs product-first.**
- Problem-first (status quo or story before product): Uber (slides 2-3), Intercom (slide 3, after team), YouTube (slide 3), Tinder (slides 2-4 as a character story), LinkedIn (slide 4, after the thesis).
- Product-first: Foursquare (definition on slide 2, screenshot on slide 3), Mint (mockup on slide 2), Buffer (trend, then question, then the product screenshot on slide 4).
- Trend or macro-claim first: Buffer ("Social, the most important trend"), Facebook (press quote).
- Split is about 5 problem-first to 3 product-first. The two most consumer-product-like decks (Foursquare, Tinder) are very visual; the two B2B or infrastructure decks (Intercom, Uber) are text-led.

**Pre-traction substitutes** (what each used instead of numbers):
- A "progress" checklist of concrete milestones: Uber ("Ubercab.com reserved", "Provisional patent filed", "5 advisors & 15 clients now recruited"), Intercom ("testing since Jan", private beta).
- Peer or press validation: Intercom (Jason Fried tweet), Facebook (three student-newspaper quotes), Foursquare (two merchants' tweets about "mayors").
- Named backers and operator credentials: Mint (First Round, Felicis, Ron Conway, Scott Cook), Intercom ("Launched Qwitter and Exceptional (both acquired)"), YouTube (PayPal alumni on the team slide).
- A working product or screenshots as evidence: Foursquare (13 of 15 slides), Tinder, Buffer slide 4, Mint's mockup.
- Reach metrics for early products: Buffer (800 paying users, 40% monthly growth), Facebook (70,000 users, 90M pageviews, launch dates), LinkedIn (growth versus last round's projection).
- Nobody in the seed set used a waitlist or LOI; Uber's "15 clients recruited" is the closest to a pipeline claim.

**Ask framing.** Four of the seven seed decks have no ask slide (Buffer, Foursquare, YouTube, Tinder). The three that state one do it differently:
- Intercom: amount, instrument, runway and milestones on one slide ("$600k convertible note", "18 months of runway", product-market fit, customer dev, early marketing, profitability, "Plan to raise further round in 12-18 months").
- Uber: a vague amount plus a concrete use ("Raise a few million", "buy 3 cars, develop app, Feb1st demo").
- Mint: an implied $3.0M for 15% inside a returns analysis.
- Later-round decks frame the ask as an outcome or plan: LinkedIn ("Operating Profitability in 2005"), Moz ($20-25M with a use-of-funds section).
- Pattern: when the deck has an ask, it ties money to a time-boxed milestone, not a generic "growth" claim.

**Market sizing.** Mostly top-down, with a third-party source named on the slide: Uber ($4.2B, then "SF/NYC... covers 50% of entire US market" city narrowing), Intercom ($21B, Forrester), Facebook (15M students, $85B purchasing power), Canva (four industry figures attached to a process map), LinkedIn (comparables per revenue line). Mint is the only bottom-up build (people, then prospective users, then revenue per user, then TAM). YouTube, Buffer, Foursquare and Tinder give no market number and argue timing or trend instead.

**Headline style.** Seed decks are overwhelmingly topic labels ("Problem", "Solution", "Traction", "Market Size"). Takeaway sentences are rare in the seed set and show up as exceptions: Uber slide 3's green bottom line, Foursquare slides 6 and 11, Buffer slide 2, Tinder's story slides. Takeaway-sentence titles are standard in the later decks (LinkedIn: "Strong Results With Less Than $4 Million Spent"; Moz: "Up until 2010, SEOmoz had never spent money directly to acquire customers!").

**Design.**
- Words per slide: lowest in Tinder, YouTube, Buffer, Foursquare (under about 40 words); highest in Facebook and LinkedIn (paragraph or table density).
- One idea per slide: strong in Buffer, Tinder, Foursquare, YouTube; weak in Uber, Mint, LinkedIn.
- Visuals: product screenshots dominate where the product is the argument (Foursquare, Tinder, Buffer slide 4); text and logos elsewhere. Only Buffer and Tinder use large display type.
- Few of these decks look polished by current standards. **[inferred]** Substance, not design, got them funded, but the two most memorable (Buffer, Tinder) are also the most visually clean.

**What this suggests for Spottr [inferred].** The pre-traction cases closest to Spottr (Foursquare, Intercom, Uber) combined: a concrete product walkthrough, a short "progress" slide of verifiable steps, third-party validation (a respected peer's quote, named pilots, press), and a single time-boxed ask. Strong seed decks ran 8-16 slides. The shortest ask slide (Intercom's) is the best model to copy structurally.
