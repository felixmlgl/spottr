# 01 - What YC and top seed investors say a seed deck should be

Researched 2026-10-13 for Spottr (pre-seed, ~$300k, YC W27 application due 2026-11-02, a16z / speedrun pitch).

Method notes
- Every source below was opened in this session. YC Library pages (Seibel, Harris, Hale) were pulled as raw article text, so those quotes are exact. Other pages (Sequoia, Paul Graham, speedrun Substack, Dropbox/DocSend, TechCrunch, NextView, YC video/apply pages) were read through a page-summarising fetch tool; quotes there are as returned by the tool, kept short, and should be re-checked against the page before being put on a slide or in public.
- **[inferred]** = my inference, not stated by the source.
- Stage weight: pre-seed / seed / later / all. Demo Day material is YC-batch-stage (pre-seed/seed) but about a 2.5 minute stage talk, not a read-alone deck.

## 2. Checklist of concrete, testable rules

### A. Overall structure and length

| ID | Rule | Source | Verbatim quote | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | The deck can be boiled down to 5-7 ideas you want investors to remember; every slide serves one of them | S1 | "the 5 to 7 most important ideas people should know about your startup" | pre-seed/seed |
| R02 | Demo Day (stage) deck is about 5-7 slides (2:30 talk) | S1 | "Demo Day slides ideally have only about 5-7 slides." | pre-seed/seed (stage talk) |
| R03 | Read-alone/application deck: no more than 7 slides (speedrun GP view); hard ceiling about 15 | S10 | Josh Lu: "I don't think you need more than seven slides, honestly." Marcus Segal: "Fifteen is the longest you're going to get me to click through." | pre-seed/seed |
| R04 | DocSend's 2015 seed study suggested 10 slides for a seed deck (recommended length, not a measured optimum) | S18 | (paraphrase) recommended seed deck length: 10 slides | seed (2015) |
| R05 | Each topic gets one slide; if more, a "set" of at most 3. Only the title is guaranteed a single slide | S3 | "You probably don't want any set here where n > 3. This is a seed deck, remember." | seed |
| R06 | Focus on narrative, clarity and concision; no market treatise or world philosophy | S3 | "Focus on narrative. The rest is commentary." | seed |
| R07 | Reason for brevity: at seed there is little real detail; pretending otherwise muddles the story | S3 | "there isn't very much meaningful detail to explore for most seed stage companies" | seed |
| R08 | YC seed order: Title, Problem, Solution, Traction, Unique insight, Business model, Market, Team, Ask | S3 | (slide sequence of the template) | seed |
| R09 | Sequoia order: Purpose, Problem, Solution, Why now, Market, Competition, Business model, Team, Financials, Vision | S8 | (ten section headings) | seed to growth |

| R11 | DocSend (2015): the order that performed best closely matched Sequoia's, with product and team moved earlier | S18 | (paraphrase; full order not spelled out) | seed (2015) |

### B. Title slide and one-liner

| ID | Rule | Source | Verbatim quote | Stage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R17 | Title slide = company name + one-line description of what you do | S3 | "the name of your company, and a one line description of what you do" | seed |
