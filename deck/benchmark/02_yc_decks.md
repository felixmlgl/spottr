# Early decks of YC companies that became large

Research date: 2026-10-13. Purpose: benchmark for Spottr's YC W27 and a16z decks.

## Method and honesty notes

- Every deck below was opened slide by slide. Slides were viewed as images (screenshots in the built-in browser) from the hosting page or from a PDF viewer. Headlines are quoted from what is visible on the slide. Small body text was sometimes only partly legible; where a number is unclear it is not given.
- Source hosts: Alexander Jarvis (alexanderjarvis.com) hosts per-slide images of Airbnb, Coinbase, Dropbox, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Brex. BestPitchDeck (bestpitchdeck.com) embeds PDFs of GitLab and Replit.
- Labels like "Problem" in the Airbnb deck are the slide's own headline (checked on the image). Reviewer commentary on those pages was used only for round/date/amount context, never for slide content.
- Anything marked **[inferred]** is my reading, not stated on a slide.
- Decks covered: 8. Six are seed / Demo Day / pre-seed material, two are later rounds (Mixpanel Series B, Brex Series B) kept as contrast.

## 1. Airbnb (AirBed&Breakfast), seed deck

- **Round / year:** Seed. Source page lists a $600k seed (April 2009, Sequoia). BestPitchDeck dates the deck 2008. The deck itself asks for a $500K angel round. The market-validation slide cites data for "07/09 – 07/16", so the deck was built around mid-2008 **[inferred]**.
- **Source opened:** https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/airbnb-seed-pitch-deck/ (14 slide images, all viewed).
- **Length:** 14 slides.

**Slide order (headline verbatim):**
1. "Welcome" (logo, "Book rooms with locals, rather than hotels.")
2. "Problem"
3. "Solution"
4. "Market Validation"
5. "Market Size"
6. "Product"
7. "Business Model"
8. "Adoption Strategy"
9. "Competition"
10. "Competitive Advantages"
11. "Team"
12. "Press"
13. "User Testimonials"
14. "Financial"

- **The hook (first 3 slides):** Logo plus tagline, then three bold lines of problem ("Price is a important concern...", "Hotels leave you disconnected from the city and its culture.", "No easy way exists to book a room with a local or become a host."), then the solution as three big buttons: "SAVE MONEY / MAKE MONEY / SHARE CULTURE". Problem and solution take 2 slides, each with a rule of three.
- **One-liner:** "Book rooms with locals, rather than hotels."
- **Traction / lack of it:** No own traction numbers. The deck substitutes (a) "Market Validation": two big numbers from adjacent products, "660,000" total users (Couchsurfing.com) and "50,000" temporary housing listings per week in the US (Craigslist), (b) a "Press" slide with four quote bubbles (Webware, Josh Spear, Mashable, Springwise), (c) a "User Testimonials" slide with four photo-and-quote bubbles. Team slide also quotes a prior app with 75,000 users.
- **Market sizing:** Bottom-up-ish funnel in trips, not dollars. "2 Billion+" trips booked worldwide (Total Available Market) to "560M" budget and online (Serviceable Available Market) to "84M" trips with Airbnb (share of market, labelled as a 15% share **[partly legible]**). Business Model slide then multiplies: 84M trips x "$25" avg fee ("$80/night @ 3 nights" at 10% commission) = "$2.1B" revenue "Projected by 2011". Every number carries a source footnote.
- **Ask:** Yes, last slide "Financial": "We are looking for 12 months financing to reach 80,000 transactions" ; "$500K" angel round, "80K" trips at avg $25 fee, "$2M" revenue over 12 months. Clear amount, runway, milestone and revenue outcome on one slide. No use-of-funds breakdown.
- **Headline style:** 100% topic labels ("Problem", "Solution", "Market Size"). The information is in the body, not the title.
- **Design:** Light-blue template, big numerals and circles, 3 items per slide almost everywhere, one idea per slide, little paragraph text. Footer with company address/URL on early slides.
- Other content: "Adoption Strategy" slide (events, partnerships, Craigslist dual-posting) gives the go-to-market; "Competition" is a 2-axis map; "Competitive Advantages" is six labelled boxes.

## 2. Coinbase, Demo Day slides (seed)

- **Round / year:** Seed, 2012 (source table: $600k, Sep 2012). The deck is Brian Armstrong's YC Demo Day slides; the source quotes his email: "We don't have a deck but here are my slides from demo day". So this is a Demo Day deck used as the seed deck.
- **Source opened:** https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/coinbase-pitch-deck-raise-seed-capital-investment/ (11 slide images, all viewed).
- **Length:** 11 slides.

**Slide order (headline verbatim):**
1. Cover: logo, "Your hosted bitcoin wallet" (with desktop and phone screenshots)
2. "Bitcoin: A New Digital Currency" (Instant / International / No Transaction Fees)
3. "Bitcoin: A New Digital Currency" (globe visual)
4. "Bitcoin Growth" ("$2 million USD per day in transaction volume", line chart)
5. "Early Adopters" (word cloud: international payments, virtual goods, games, developing world, peer-to-peer, micro-transactions, remittance, e-commerce)
6. "Too Difficult To Use" ("Current Tools" screenshots vs "Coinbase")
7. "Coinbase: A Hosted Bitcoin Wallet"
8. No headline: "Coinbase : Bitcoin / iTunes : MP3"
9. "Coinbase Signups" ("20% Daily")
10. "Coinbase Transactions" ("$65,000 USD in the first 5 weeks")
11. Closing: logo, "Your hosted bitcoin wallet", contact email (redacted on the image)

- **The hook:** Cover with product screenshots, then two slides that explain the category (Bitcoin is instant, international, no fees; globe visual), then a market-growth chart. The problem ("Too Difficult To Use") arrives only at slide 6, after the category case. The product is visible on slide 1.
- **One-liner:** "Your hosted bitcoin wallet".
- **Traction:** Two dedicated slides after the pitch: daily signups growing "20% Daily" and "$65,000 USD in the first 5 weeks" of transaction volume. Growth chart for the whole market (slide 4) acts as "why now". Traction is brand-new (weeks), shown as charts.
- **Market sizing:** No TAM. Market is shown as a transaction-volume chart for Bitcoin ($2M per day) plus a list of early-adopter segments (slide 5). Analogy slide "Coinbase : Bitcoin :: iTunes : MP3" does the positioning.
- **Ask:** No ask slide. Closes on contact email.
- **Headline style:** Mixed: topic labels ("Bitcoin Growth", "Early Adopters") and short claims ("Too Difficult To Use"). One slide has no headline, just the analogy.
- **Design:** Extremely sparse, 3 to 13 words per slide, default grey gradient template, large screenshots and one chart per slide.

## 3. Dropbox, seed deck

- **Round / year:** $1.2M seed, Sequoia, 2007 (per source page). BestPitchDeck calls it pre-seed 2007. Slide 3 says "It's 2007", slide 17 cites YC ranking.

2. "Storage is a mess" (photo of a cluttered room)
3. "It's 2007, and it's *still* a pain to…" (4 bullets)
4. "What are people doing now?" (email attachments, USB drives, browser uploads, piecemeal solutions)
5. "In a perfect world…"
6. "Dropbox" (4 sub-bullets, "It just works")
7. "Demo" (screenshot collage)
8. "Why now?"
9. "Why better?"
10. "Competitors" (feature matrix vs Carbonite/Mozy, Foldershare, box.net: Sync, Backup, Sharing, OS integration, Web access, Versioning)
11. "What did they screw up?"
12. "Technical advantages"
13. "Team" (Drew Houston, CEO; Arash Ferdowsi, CTO; photo "Moving into our first office, 5/14/07")
14. "Business model"
15. "Platform"
16. "Customer acquisition"
17. No headline: "Ranked #1 among summer YC startups:" plus a quote and VentureBeat link

- **The hook:** Logo and tagline, then an emotional image with four words ("Storage is a mess"), then a 2007-dated pain list. Three slides, zero product so far. Product appears on slide 6, demo on slide 7.
- **One-liner:** "Moving the world's files". Solution slide ends "It just works".
- **Traction / lack of it:** No usage numbers at all. Substitutes: a live "Demo" slide (the demo is the proof), social proof from being "Ranked #1 among summer YC startups", team slide, viral-loop argument on "Customer acquisition" ("Viral elements: file sharing, shared folders, photo/media galleries").

Business model: "Individuals: freemium", "SMB: shared folder per-seat license".
- **Ask:** No ask slide.
- **Headline style:** Mix. Some are takeaway sentences or questions ("Storage is a mess", "What did they screw up?", "Why now?", "Why better?"); others are labels ("Competitors", "Team", "Business model"). The questions read like a spoken narrative.
- **Design:** White background, bullets of 4 or fewer, short lines (about 10 words max), large type, very few images. Utilitarian.

# Patterns across decks

Counts below use all 8 opened decks unless stated; "seed set" means decks 1 to 6.

**Length**
- Slide counts: GitLab 8, Coinbase 11, Amplitude 12, Mixpanel 12, Airbnb 14, Dropbox 17, Brex 19, Replit 21 (19 distinct). Median 13 for all 8 and 13 for the seed set. Range 8 to 21. The shortest (GitLab, Coinbase) were Demo Day slides built for a talk **[inferred]**.

**Common slide order (seed set)**
1. Cover with logo and one-line description (Airbnb, Coinbase, Dropbox, GitLab, Replit, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Brex: all 8 open with a logo cover, but only 6 put a line on it: Airbnb, Coinbase, Dropbox, GitLab, Replit, Amplitude; Mixpanel and Brex are logo only).
2. Problem as slide 2 or 3 (Airbnb, Dropbox, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Brex).
3. Solution or product (Airbnb 3, Dropbox 6, Mixpanel 4, Coinbase 7).
4. Proof/market validation, then product/demo, then competition, advantages, team, then a closing business/financial slide (Airbnb: Market Validation 4, Market Size 5, Product 6, Business Model 7, Adoption 9 to 10, Team 11, Financial 14; Dropbox: Demo 7, Why now 8, Competitors 10, Team 13, Business model 14).
- Team is usually late (Airbnb 11 of 14, Dropbox 13 of 17, Amplitude none, GitLab none, Coinbase none). Exceptions: Replit (team at 6, founder story first) and Brex (team at 2).

**Lead with problem vs product (first three slides after the cover)**
- Problem first: 5 of 8, Airbnb (slide 2), Dropbox (2), Amplitude (2), Mixpanel (2), Brex (3, after team).
- Product or category first: 1 of 8, Coinbase (cover shows product; slides 2 to 5 explain Bitcoin; problem at 6).
- Proof or founders first, no problem slide at all: 2 of 8, GitLab (customer logos at 2, "bootstrapped profitable" at 4), Replit (founder background at 2 to 5).

**How pre-traction decks substitute for traction**
- Adjacent-market numbers: Airbnb "Market Validation" (Couchsurfing 660,000 users; Craigslist 50,000 weekly listings). Coinbase market volume chart ("$2 million USD per day").
- Early-growth charts, tiny but dated: Coinbase "20% Daily" signups and "$65,000 USD in the first 5 weeks"; Amplitude "Growth (pre-launch)" "43% month on month growth in 2013"; Brex "Growing Volume In Referral Only, Private Beta".
- Press, testimonials, tweets, photos: Airbnb "Press" and "User Testimonials"; Replit "Users & Testimonials", "Social Media Love", Hour of Code photo.
- Live demo or product screens as proof: Dropbox "Demo" (slide 7); Replit "Quick demo"; Amplitude slides 4 to 6.
- Rankings and credentials: Dropbox "Ranked #1 among summer YC startups"; Airbnb team slide credentials; Replit founder slides; Brex team and investors.
- Customers or logos once they exist: GitLab (100,000 organizations, $1M run rate), Brex (customer logos on slides 10 to 13).
- Honest labelling is allowed: Amplitude's chart is titled "(pre-launch)".

**Market sizing**
- Only 2 of 8 give dollar or unit sizing: Airbnb (bottom-up funnel in trips: 2B+ to 560M to 84M, times $25 avg fee = $2.1B) and Brex (top-down nested circles: $101B to $25.6B to $5.2B).
- 6 of 8 give no number. They use a growth chart or a why-now argument instead: Coinbase ($2M/day volume chart), Amplitude (industry charts), Dropbox ("Why now?"), GitLab, Replit ("Thesis"), Mixpanel.

**Ask framing**
- Only 1 of 8 has an explicit ask slide: Airbnb "Financial" ($500K angel round for 12 months, to reach 80,000 transactions and $2M revenue; ask is tied to a milestone and to revenue).
- Mixpanel B shows "Financing history" and an expansion plan but no amount. Brex, Coinbase, Dropbox, GitLab, Replit, Amplitude: no ask slide. The ask in these decks was most likely spoken or sent in the email **[inferred]**.

**Headline style**
- Topic labels dominate the seed set: Airbnb (all 14), Replit, Amplitude (with a "Company:" prefix), Coinbase (mostly).
- Takeaway sentences: GitLab (every slide: "10x more affordable", "bootstrapped profitable team of 10"), Brex ("Entrepreneurs Can't Get Credit Cards", "Customers Exhibit 'Negative Churn'"), Dropbox ("Storage is a mess", "What did they screw up?"), Mixpanel (full-sentence problem and solution slides under a small label).
- Takeaways appear on the slides that carry the pain (problem) or the proof (traction); labels carry procedural slides (Team, Competition).

**Word density and design**
- Sparse: GitLab (about 4 to 12 words), Coinbase (3 to 13), Airbnb (big numbers, 3 items), Mixpanel (one statement per slide).
- Medium bullets: Dropbox (4 bullets of about 10 words), Amplitude (bullet and screenshot).
- Text-heavy: Replit (small grey body copy).
- Common visuals: one chart per traction slide, one screenshot per product slide, logo rows as proof. One idea per slide holds in every deck except Amplitude (3 near-duplicate advantage slides) and Replit (duplicated build slides).

**Takeaways for Spottr [inferred]**
- Closest analogues for a pre-revenue hardware-free product: Airbnb (market proxies plus press and testimonials), Amplitude (pre-launch growth chart, demo screens), Coinbase (signups and early volume in weeks), Replit (organic MAU).
- The two decks with a measurable early curve (GitLab, Coinbase) are the shortest. A visible curve lets the deck stay under about 12 slides.
- Put an explicit ask only if the deck is to be read without you: only Airbnb had one on a slide. This is a gap worth deciding on deliberately.
